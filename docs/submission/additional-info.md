## What specific problem does your solution address, and who is affected?

Maize-yard supervisors need to coordinate limited drying space, changing weather and moisture checks across farmers' lots, so each shift knows what work comes next and what happened to the grain.

## How did you use Conduit@Empathy data in your solution?

Kavu processes 18,364 unique JKUAT Conduit observations, removes 2,825 overlapping rows and retains traceable sources. Temperature, humidity and the primary rain gauge inform explainable drying rules. Those rules combine with each lot's measured moisture, deadline and available yard capacity to recommend work.

The effect is visible: with the same example inventory and capacity, September 12 conditions allocate 2,700 kg outdoors. September 15's humid observations or the September 8 data gap allocate zero. Historical replay makes that comparison reproducible. Operators then create jobs, record completion and add a new moisture reading to the same lot history.

## What impact could your solution create?

Kavu could help a cooperative use drying windows more effectively, hand work between shifts clearly and maintain reliable moisture records for many farmers' lots. The immediate value to test is less time reconstructing lot histories, more consistent rechecks and clearer decisions about outdoor handling or mechanical drying.

The ledger provides an explicit reference-cost comparison. For the demonstration lot, 1.8 tonnes and a 3.2 percentage-point moisture reduction correspond to about KSh 2,176 at NCPB's published tonne tariff. Field use must establish actual costs and benefits. The proposed pilot will measure handling effort, rechecks, interruptions and documented charges before making savings or food-loss claims.

## What is the next step for your project after the hackathon?

Recruit up to three maize yards and a local grain-quality professional for a supervised pilot. Observe their current workflow, review sampling and operating thresholds, then compare recommendations alongside experienced operators before introducing task coordination.

Measure daily use, handover time, rechecks, support effort and actual costs. Test whether the buyer wants to renew at the starting hypothesis of KSh 2,500 per active month per site. Expand site by site once local data relevance and useful operation are established.
